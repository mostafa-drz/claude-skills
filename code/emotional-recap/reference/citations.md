# Citations reference

Curated scientific references the report draws from. The skill picks a subset per `citation_depth` setting:

- `minimal` — 1-line refs inline, no full list at bottom
- `standard` — APA-style inline citations + a 5-item reference list
- `academic` — full reference list at bottom (~10 items), inline APA throughout

All entries below are real, peer-reviewed or widely-cited works. Do not invent citations; if you don't have the relevant ref here for a specific claim, drop the claim rather than fabricate.

---

## Emotion theory & taxonomy

**Plutchik, R. (1980).** _Emotion: A Psychoevolutionary Synthesis._ Harper & Row.
The wheel of emotions. Foundational for any discrete-emotion tagging.

**Ekman, P. (1992).** An argument for basic emotions. _Cognition & Emotion_, 6(3-4), 169-200.
The six basic emotions framework.

**Russell, J. A. (1980).** A circumplex model of affect. _Journal of Personality and Social Psychology_, 39(6), 1161-1178.
Valence × arousal dimensional model.

**Posner, J., Russell, J. A., & Peterson, B. S. (2005).** The circumplex model of affect: An integrative approach to affective neuroscience, cognitive development, and psychopathology. _Development and Psychopathology_, 17(3), 715-734.
Modern integration of Russell's model with neuroscience.

**Barrett, L. F. (2017).** _How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain._ Houghton Mifflin Harcourt.
The constructionist counterweight — emotion categories are not biologically fixed. Cite in caveats to keep the report intellectually honest.

## Linguistic markers & sentiment

**Pennebaker, J. W. (2003).** Telling stories: The health benefits of narrative. _Literature and Medicine_, 19(1), 3-18.
Linguistic markers of psychological state.

**Tausczik, Y. R., & Pennebaker, J. W. (2010).** The psychological meaning of words: LIWC and computerized text analysis methods. _Journal of Language and Social Psychology_, 29(1), 24-54.
The methods paper for LIWC-style analysis used throughout this skill.

**Rude, S., Gortner, E.-M., & Pennebaker, J. (2004).** Language use of depressed and depression-vulnerable college students. _Cognition & Emotion_, 18(8), 1121-1133.
The empirical basis for "rising 1st-person singular pronouns track with rumination / depressive cognition."

**Mohammad, S. M., & Turney, P. D. (2013).** Crowdsourcing a word–emotion association lexicon. _Computational Intelligence_, 29(3), 436-465.
The NRC Emotion Lexicon — basis for Plutchik-tag word lists.

## Wellbeing, flow, and positive psychology

**Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990).** _Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience._ Harper & Row.
Used to reframe productive frustration. Engaged struggle ≠ distress.

**Fredrickson, B. L. (2001).** The role of positive emotions in positive psychology: The broaden-and-build theory of positive emotions. _American Psychologist_, 56(3), 218-226.
Why the "bright spots" section is required, not optional.

**Seligman, M. E. P., & Csikszentmihalyi, M. (2000).** Positive psychology: An introduction. _American Psychologist_, 55(1), 5-14.
The umbrella reference for the supportive framing of this skill.

## Sleep, circadian, and late-night work

**Walker, M. (2017).** _Why We Sleep: Unlocking the Power of Sleep and Dreams._ Scribner.
The pop-science synthesis. Cited when late-night sessions correlate with negative-valence drops.

**Yoo, S.-S., Gujar, N., Hu, P., Jolesz, F. A., & Walker, M. P. (2007).** The human emotional brain without sleep — a prefrontal amygdala disconnect. _Current Biology_, 17(20), R877-R878.
The empirical case: one night of sleep deprivation amplifies negative emotional reactivity by ~60%.

## Micro-experiments & actionable anchors (cited in §04 Worth trying)

These references back the **Worth trying** section. Each is a real peer-reviewed paper supporting a small, testable behaviour change. The skill must NOT generate Worth-trying items without a citation from this list or the lists above.

**Ariga, A., & Lleras, A. (2011).** Brief and rare mental "breaks" keep you focused: Deactivation and reactivation of task goals preempt vigilance decrements. _Cognition_, 118(3), 439-443.
Foundational for the "brief break restores attention" recommendation. Use when a frustration streak or attention-residue pattern is detected.

**Leroy, S. (2009).** Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks. _Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes_, 109(2), 168-181.
Attention residue across task switches. Use when single-project absorption + cross-project context-switching is detected.

**Lieberman, M. D., Eisenberger, N. I., Crockett, M. J., Tom, S. M., Pfeifer, J. H., & Way, B. M. (2007).** Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. _Psychological Science_, 18(5), 421-428.
The neuroscientific basis for "naming the emotion reduces its hold." Use when low emotional-vocabulary patterns surface — the action is to name the felt state, not to suppress it.

**Mauss, I. B., Tamir, M., Anderson, C. L., & Savino, N. S. (2011).** Can seeking happiness make people unhappy? Paradoxical effects of valuing happiness. _Emotion_, 11(4), 807-815.
Cite when patterns suggest the user is grading their own emotional state — caveat against making wellbeing itself a target.

**Sonnentag, S., & Fritz, C. (2007).** The recovery experience questionnaire: Development and validation of a measure for assessing recuperation and unwinding from work. _Journal of Occupational Health Psychology_, 12(3), 204-221.
Use when end-of-day patterns suggest poor detachment from work (e.g. late-night negative valence after intense daytime sessions).

**Killingsworth, M. A., & Gilbert, D. T. (2010).** A wandering mind is an unhappy mind. _Science_, 330(6006), 932.
Cite for present-moment-attention suggestions when mind-wandering / task-switching is implicated.

**Maslach, C., & Leiter, M. P. (2016).** Understanding the burnout experience: recent research and its implications for psychiatry. _World Psychiatry_, 15(2), 103-111.
Reference for sustained negative-quadrant streaks across many sessions.

Framework for "demands vs resources" reframe when the user is consistently in high-arousal-negative.

## Caveats / scope-limiting

**Hutto, C. J., & Gilbert, E. (2014).** VADER: A parsimonious rule-based model for sentiment analysis of social media text. _Proceedings of ICWSM 2014._
Cite when explaining limits of text-sentiment analysis (sarcasm, brevity, domain shift).

**Barrett, L. F., Adolphs, R., Marsella, S., Martinez, A. M., & Pollak, S. D. (2019).** Emotional expressions reconsidered: Challenges to inferring emotion from human facial movements. _Psychological Science in the Public Interest_, 20(1), 1-68.
Cite (alongside Barrett 2017) when noting that the report is one signal, not a verdict.

## Selection rules

When choosing citations for a report:

1. **Always cite the framework being used** (Plutchik / Ekman / Russell / Pennebaker).
2. **For each pattern flagged**, cite the supporting paper from this list.
3. **Always include one caveat citation** (VADER or Barrett 2019) in the caveats section.
4. **At `academic` depth**, also include one positive-psychology citation (Fredrickson) in the bright-spots section.
5. Order the reference list **alphabetically by first author** in the final report.

Do not exceed `citation_depth` budget — fewer well-placed citations beats a wall of references.
